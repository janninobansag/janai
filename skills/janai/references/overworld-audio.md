# Overworld Audio design reference

Use this reference when shaping a creative-studio portfolio or a media-led experience. It draws interaction and content cues from [Overworld Audio](https://overworldaudio.com/), an audio studio site; it is not a universal portfolio template.

## What to take from the reference

- The site presents its work as explorable projects, with a short positioning statement and a direct path into the portfolio.
- Navigation includes Projects, Studio, and Contact, giving visitors clear routes between work samples, capabilities, and the next step.
- The home experience invites visitors to drag or scroll and select markers. A sound on/off control makes audio an explicit part of the experience.
- Studio content explains the broader service system—sound design, music, implementation, and audio direction—instead of treating the work as a gallery alone.

## Apply the ideas with care

- Let the studio's actual work lead. Use immersive presentation when it helps visitors understand the craft, context, or feeling of each project.
- Make experimental navigation discoverable and provide a straightforward route to projects, studio information, and contact. Support keyboard, touch, and scrolling input.
- Keep sound opt-in, communicate its state, and ensure the core experience still works when sound is off or unavailable.
- Treat motion and media as part of the story, with responsive layouts, reduced-motion support, useful loading behavior, and accessible text alternatives.
- Match the amount of interaction to the content and audience. A conventional portfolio may work better when visitors need to scan, compare, or reach information quickly.
- Use the reference for storytelling and interaction ideas, not as a blueprint. Preserve the project's own identity, assets, and information architecture.
