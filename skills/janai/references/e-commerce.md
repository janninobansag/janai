# E-commerce design reference

Use this reference when shaping a storefront, product listing, or product-detail experience. It captures useful cues from one sportswear storefront concept; it is not a universal e-commerce template.

## Inspiration

[E-commerce Website by Ronas IT | UI/UX Team on Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/shots/22402874-E-commerce-Website) presents a sneaker and sportswear store. Its description calls out product imagery, size selection, price, ratings, and reviews, using a light palette with dark elements to keep attention on the merchandise.

## Apply the cues to the product

- Let accurate product photography and the information needed to compare items lead the page. Keep decoration and color subordinate when that helps people inspect the merchandise.
- Show useful product facts such as name, price, and relevant options on listing cards. Use size selectors for products that have sizes; choose different variant controls for other products.
- Put ratings, review counts, discounts, availability, shipping, and return details into the design only when the product data and business rules support them. Never invent them to fill a layout.
- Make option states, unavailable variants, add-to-cart feedback, and purchase controls understandable with a keyboard, assistive technology, and touch. Preserve visible focus and responsive product imagery.
- Choose navigation, filters, sorting, and detail-page structure from the actual catalog and buying task. A sneaker-store example does not determine how a grocery, furniture, or digital-goods shop should work.
- Use the reference to guide hierarchy and restraint; create an original composition with the store's own brand, content, and supplied assets. Do not reproduce the shot's artwork or exact layout.
